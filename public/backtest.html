How it works

Funds copied: Pershing Square, Berkshire Hathaway, Appaloosa, Tiger Global, Coatue, Third Point and Duquesne. Quant and multi-manager firms (Renaissance, Citadel, Millennium, Bridgewater, Point72) and Soros are left out: their 13F positions are often hedges or short-term trades rather than ideas worth copying a quarter later.
  • Trade date: the first trading day after each 13F deadline (Feb 14, May 15, Aug 14, Nov 14, moved to Monday when it falls on a weekend). Only filings published by the deadline are used, so nothing is used before it was public.
  • Consensus 10: the stocks held by the most of these funds, counting every holding in each filing. Ties go to the larger combined value.
  • Best ideas 10: each fund's largest position, then each fund's second largest, and so on (ordered by % of that fund's portfolio) until there are 10 different stocks.
  • Fresh buys 10: positions that weren't in the fund's previous filing, ranked by % of the buying fund's portfolio. Empty slots are held in SPY.
  • Portfolio: 10% in each stock, held until the next trade date. A stock that stops trading (for example after a takeover) is held at its last price, like cash, until then. Index funds and ETFs that appear in filings are skipped.
  • Live tracking: from the first trade date after this page launched, the same rules keep running forward as paper portfolios. That part isn't a backtest: the picks were fixed before the returns happened.
